# Week 7 — Wireframes & Prototype Handoff

## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Project Overview

Briefly summarize the Week 6 problem you are carrying forward.

The three problems I'm focusing on in this design might seem a little different, I based these on the feedback I recieved. First, trail measurements, I seperated the distance the trail is from you and the length of the trail in hopes of being able to differenciate them easier. I removed the categories, and focused more on time and length of the trails, so that beginners can choose something shorter. The last problem was the amount of clickable objects, I liked the hamburger menu shown in the provided video, so I thought to use that in the desktop design as well so that the user, Maya, can focus solely on the cta button shown on the landing page.

## 2. Three Design Requirements

Translate your **top three Week 6 priorities** into exactly three interface requirements.

### Requirement 1

**Week 6 problem:** Trail measurements
**Design requirement:** Seperate the distance from the user and the trail length to prevent confusion.
**How this helps Maya:** Maya would be able to tell how far the trail is, and how long it would take to complete.

### Requirement 2

**Week 6 problem:** Removed Easy, Medium, Hard Categories.
**Design requirement:** focused more on length and time of a trail.
**How this helps Maya:** Maya is looking for a trail that will last less than three hours, if we do this, she'll be able to find a trail that meets her requirements.

### Requirement 3

**Week 6 problem:** Clickable objects
**Design requirement:** have less objects to distract the user.
**How this helps Maya:** Allows Maya to focus on choosing her trail.

## 5. Prototype Flow

Describe the user task your prototype demonstrates.

**Starting point:** She jumps into the website hoping to find a short trail to go on in less than 3 hours.
**User action:** She clicks the Find a Trail button
**System/interface response:** The system brings up trails one at a time, allowing her to click through them
**End state:** She stops on the trail she believes to be the best for her.

---

## 6. Design Rationale

Document approximately three important decisions.

### Decision 1

**Problem:** clickable distractive buttons
**Design response:** I stored the nav into a hamburger menu similar to the phone version.
**Why:** So that Maya can focus on finding her trail in time, and she doesn't waste time looking through the site since she is on a time crunch.

### Decision 2

**Problem:** Easy, Medium, Hard Categories.
**Design response:** Removed from design, went more in depth about time, distance and elevation.
**Why:** In order to provide the proper information for those researching hiking.

### Decision 3

**Problem:** Trail Measurements
**Design response:** Seperated the lengths from each other and properly labeled which was which.
**Why:** both seem like they'd be important to the user, so I wanted to keep them but have them be less confusing for her.

## 7. Accessibility Notes

Document at least two accessibility decisions you planned before development.

### Accessibility Decision 1

A central cta button, that allows Maya to focus and percieve one trail at a time, while giving her all the necessary information she needs to make a decision.

### Accessibility Decision 2

Changed the trail distance information so that everyone can easily understand the distance, length of the trail and how long it may take to complete.
